# Healthcare-Workforce-Attrition-Analysis
## Overview
This project explores healthcare workforce attrition using **Python** and **Pandas**. By analysing internal HR and operational datasets, the goal of this project is to answer two core questions: 
1. **Why are employees leaving the organisation?**
2. **What targeted, data-backed interventions can be implemented to improve retention and safeguard the quality of care?**

---

## Key Findings & Insights
* **Departmental Disparities:** Turnover is heavily concentrated rather than evenly distributed, with Cardiology (13.9%) and Maternity (12.3%) experiencing the highest attrition rates.
* **The Overtime Impact:** Employees working regular overtime face an attrition rate of **29.2%**, compared to just **5%** for those who do not—highlighting operational burnout as a primary risk factor.
* **Training & Development:** Staff who received zero hours of formal training in the past year recorded a **21.3%** turnover rate, proving that professional stagnation heavily influences exits.
* **Demographic Profile:** High-risk leavers tend to be younger (median age 30), earn lower monthly incomes ($2.5K–$4.8K), have longer commutes, and typically depart within their first 1 to 4 years.

---

## Strategic Recommendations
Based on the exploratory data analysis (EDA), the project outlines four key intervention pillars:
1. **Reduce Overtime:** Rebalance shift rotas and implement strict monitoring on sustained overtime in high-strain clinical units.
2. **Improve Employee Wellbeing:** Establish structured feedback mechanisms to catch and address work-life balance concerns early.
3. **Strengthen Early Career Support:** Introduce robust onboarding, formal mentoring, and guaranteed training pathways during an employee's initial years.
4. **Target High-Risk Departments:** Direct retention budgets and interventions precisely where turnover is highest (Cardiology and Nursing teams).
